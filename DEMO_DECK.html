An automated tester for CDUI's six main screens

It opens CDUI in the browser the way a person would, works through the screens the MDG team uses every day, and reports what it saw — with a screenshot wherever something was wrong.

What it checks

  • Every screen signs in and loads.
  • Ticket List — searching and filtering tickets, paging, opening a ticket.
  • Create Stewardship Ticket — the form accepts good input and blocks bad input, for every ticket type and identifier type.
  • Party Search — lookup by ID, name, site or DUNS, and every tab of the party details.
  • ODW ID Translation — DCN, UCID and APID translated in both directions.

How it works

  • Runs in Edge with the normal Dell sign-in. Nobody watches it.
  • When a check fails it saves a screenshot of the screen at that moment and writes down the steps to reproduce.
  • Produces one report — green or red per screen — and e-mails it.
  • Starts with one command: anyone on the team, or a nightly schedule.

What it never does

  • Change data in CDUI. Every edit would raise a real MDG stewardship ticket, so the routine run only reads.
  • Hide a failure. A screen that loads blank is recorded as a failure, not refreshed until it works.
  • Depend on one person. The same run works from any machine with a Dell login.
  • Replace the manual testers. It takes the repetitive pass off their plate, so their time goes to exploratory testing and new features.
Built in four weeks, since 19 August, on the same foundation as our API automation — one team maintains both, and the test data comes from the real systems, not from hand-typed values.

What we get from it

Six things the team could not do before, or could only do by giving up a tester for a day.

Regression without losing a tester for a day

A full pass by hand is 8 to 12 hours. Automated, it is about 30 minutes and the person is free to do other work. It can run before every release, or every night.

Every problem arrives with proof

Steps, what was expected, what actually happened, and a screenshot at the moment it went wrong. Developers stop asking “how do I reproduce this?”

An independent check on reported defects

The ten defects the manual team raised in August were all re-tested within a day — all ten are real — and for three of them we found the exact spot for developers to look, which the tickets did not say.

Know the moment a fix has landed

Each confirmed defect has a check that stays red while the bug is open and turns green when it is fixed. No re-test needed, and no relying on a ticket status.

Catches what people retry past

Screens that load blank get recorded, not refreshed. That is how we know Account Search has not rendered in any run since August — which nobody had logged.

Repeatable by anyone

One command. The result does not depend on who started it. It already runs unattended on the team's GitLab runner.

In the demo (about two minutes): the browser opens on its own and runs six checks — a ticket search, filling in a stewardship ticket form (cancelled, nothing is created) and ODW translation. The report then opens by itself.
